Supervised fine-tuning, i.e., distillation, lets another model reproduce one's capabilities more easily. And somehow that's now a national-security problem when the student might become a competitor.

OpenAI’s latest anti-distillation post is showing that exact reasoning traces can transfer more cyber capability under some conditions. Like... No? This is not connected to restricting distillation making the public safer, considering its dual-use nature.[1] A capability curve cannot make that argument only for you.

And, besides those "cyber stuffs", distillation has been used to teach safer reasoning and reduce jailbreak success, which, to them, is a way of safe deployment of intelligence.[2] And, they did... What? Made an internal Helpful-only on purpose, and used that to prove that OpenAI should be in charge of controlling the learner...?

(Yes, I'm using this again) Funnily enough, OpenAI launched its own distillation product.[3] Its usage policy restricts supplying training material for models intended as alternatives or substitutes, extending to labels, rubrics and rewards... whilst giving users the fullest permission over the model's outputs.[4] Whatsoever might be a business protection whilst it was not, it is not, and it will never be national-security and public-benefit stuff.

Claude Opus and Fable refused parts of the forensic work; Hugging Face used GLM-5.2 locally to help investigate.[5] The gated lab supplied the agents that caused the damage. An open model helped the target defend itself. Count that, too, when calculating “safety.”

(also, OpenAI limited the public report of raw CoT from that... for "IP reasons" btw)[6]
